# Interview notes: Acme Analytics (fictional) — data integration rollout

All names, companies, and figures in this file are invented for a synthetic example.

## Company background

Acme Analytics is a fictional mid-sized retailer of garden supplies with 38 stores and an online shop. The data team has four people: a manager, two analysts, and one engineer. Before 2025 every report was built by hand in spreadsheets. The nightly sales file arrived by email at 6 a.m. and was copied into a workbook. The manager said: 'We spent Monday mornings checking whether yesterday's numbers were yesterday's numbers.'

## The trigger

In March 2025 the online shop moved to a new platform. Its export format changed without notice. For eleven days the Monday sales report was wrong and nobody noticed. A store manager caught it when a product that had sold out still showed stock. The data manager called this 'the eleven days' in the interview and said it was the moment the company agreed to buy an integration tool.

## What they bought

The team chose a data-integration product the interviewer referred to as the Vendor. It connects the shop platform, the point-of-sale system, and the warehouse system to one warehouse database. The team evaluated three products over six weeks. The deciding factor was that the Vendor's connector for the shop platform existed and worked in the trial; the other two needed custom code. Price was second. The manager said the licence was 'about what we spent on one contractor month.'

## Rollout

The rollout took nine weeks, which was three weeks longer than the plan. The delay came from the point-of-sale system, which had two store numbers for the same store after a 2023 merger. The engineer wrote a mapping table by hand. The Vendor's support team answered within a day on every ticket, which the engineer called 'the best part.' Nobody from the Vendor visited on site.

## Results after six months

The Monday report now runs at 5 a.m. without a person. The team counts 14 reports that moved off spreadsheets. The manager estimates the team gets back about one day a week. The one number she would stand behind is 14 reports; the one-day estimate is her feeling. Stock-out detection moved from weekly to daily. Two stores changed their reorder day because of it.

## What did not work

The Vendor's dashboard product was tried for one month and dropped. The analysts preferred their existing charting tool. The manager said the Vendor 'kept trying to sell us the dashboard' and that this was 'the only thing that annoyed us.' A promised connector for the loyalty program was late by four months and is still in beta.

## Advice to peers

The manager's advice: start with one report that everybody agrees is wrong, fix that first, and do not buy the dashboard. She would buy the integration product again. She would not sign a three-year contract again, because the loyalty connector delay showed that roadmap promises slip.
